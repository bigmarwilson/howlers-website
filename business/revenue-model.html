Revenue Model

Memberships as the Core Revenue Engine

Howler’s Backyard is intentionally designed around recurring monthly memberships. This model creates predictable revenue, stronger retention, and a more durable financial foundation than visit‑based dog services.

Membership pricing is projected in the $65–$85 per‑dog monthly range, with blended averages around $75.

Day Passes

Day passes support trial visits, tourism, and occasional users. They serve as a conversion funnel into recurring memberships and help maintain accessibility for non‑members.

Private Events

Birthday parties, adoption events, breed meetups, corporate gatherings, and community rentals expand utilization during off‑peak hours. Event revenue grows as membership density increases.

Training & Specialty Programming

Puppy socials, intro sessions, enrichment classes, and low‑key training programs provide high‑margin add‑on revenue. These offerings also improve retention and deepen member engagement.

Food Truck Partnerships

Instead of operating a capital‑heavy kitchen or bar, Howler’s partners with licensed food trucks on a rotating schedule. This approach reduces labor overhead, buildout complexity, and compliance burden while still enhancing customer dwell time and event appeal.

Retail & Partnerships

Modest retail offerings and brand partnerships provide supplemental revenue without requiring significant inventory or staffing. These channels grow naturally as the member base expands.

Projected Revenue Growth

The financial model anticipates membership growth from 250–325 active members in Year 1 to 525–650 by Year 3. Total revenue is projected to grow from approximately $457,500 in Year 1 to $900,000 in Year 3.

As membership density increases, incremental revenue begins to drop through more efficiently, improving EBITDA and strengthening long‑term unit economics.
